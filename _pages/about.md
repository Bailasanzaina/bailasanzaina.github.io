# Hi, I'm Bailasan!

### I speak syntax, think in Python, and occasionally argue with algorithms.

I'm a PhD student at Georgetown University, where I study Arabic syntax and morphology and work on computational approaches to language. Most of my research involves figuring out how grammatical patterns work, how to model them, and what happens when we try to test those models on real language data.

One question I keep coming back to is: **How much of what we know about language can we actually teach a computer?**

My work ranges from studying morphosyntactic agreement to working with Arabic corpora, writing Python code, and building and evaluating computational models. I enjoy moving between linguistic theory and programming, especially when the results raise more questions than they answer.

## What I'm Working On

- **Arabic NLP:** Developing and evaluating computational approaches to Arabic language processing.
- **Syntax & Morphology:** Studying grammatical structure, particularly morphosyntactic agreement.
- **Python & Machine Learning:** Using programming and computational methods to investigate language.
- **Corpus Analysis & Annotation:** Working with linguistic datasets and structured annotations.
- **Semantic Role Labeling:** Investigating how grammatical context influences the identification of semantic roles.
- **NLP Evaluation:** Understanding what models learn, where they fail, and what those failures tell us.

## From Theory to Code

I work with **Python, R, linguistic corpora, NLP tools, machine learning methods, and computational experiments**.

What interests me most is the process of taking a linguistic question, finding a way to test it computationally, and seeing whether the results support the original analysis.
